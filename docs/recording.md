# Recording

## Recording what you see

A gem on the left hand records what the headset shows. The gem is an octahedron of glowing glass, 25 mm across (Cranpose's `SpatialGem`). It floats 5 cm above the left controller's grip and 5.5 cm out to the left of the hand, clear of the hand menu's ball. Without a controller it floats 4.5 cm above the back of the left hand. It turns slowly. Its color says what a press does:
- the theme's glass while nothing points at it: blue in the Cyan theme, violet in the Amber theme;
- green while a ray or a hand's pointer rests on it: a press starts a recording;
- red with a slow pulse while a recording runs, pointed at or not: a press stops it;
- the theme's glass, dim, while no recording can start. A press then shows the reason beside the gem for 3 s.

The gem shows in games too, as the ball does. It hides while neither the left controller nor the left hand is tracked. A hidden gem composes nothing. The hand menu's row "Record what I see" serves a user who works with the right hand only. The Mac window's button "Record what I see" serves the Mac. All three show one state, which the Mac's app owns (`app/src/recorder.rs`). The host passes it to the headset as `HostMsg::Recording` (protocol 17). A press in the headset sends the console's request `recording.toggle`. The launch extra `record_gem=false` leaves the gem out, for a measure of its cost.

**How the picture comes to the Mac.** The headset's own recorder (Meta's MetaCam) records the picture. It records the compositor's final view of one eye with no lens warp: the game or the desktop, the holograms, the controllers, the hands and the room when passthrough shows it. The headset's own recording settings set its size and rate. cranvr changes none of them. The app pushes a small program to the headset over adb (`quest-recorder/`, built by `scripts/build-quest-recorder.sh`). The program asks the recorder to start and to stop through the recorder's app actions, as the shell user. A recording therefore needs the headset on the USB cable with USB debugging, which needs Developer Mode. Without adb the gem says "Recording needs the headset on the USB cable with USB debugging". At the stop the app pulls the recorder's file. It deletes the headset's copy only once the Mac's file is safe: the pulled file has the headset's size, and the final file passed its check (its picture as long as the headset's, its sound there). The Quest's storage is small, and the Mac keeps the recording. A file that cranvr did not record, such as one of the headset's own menu, cranvr never touches.

**What the headset's recorder does.** The headset's recorder records on by itself whatever happens to the app or the cable, so the app keeps a journal of each recording beside its parts (`~/Movies/cranvr/.cranvr-STAMP/recording.json`): the headset, its videos before the start and the recording's own file. The app reads the recorder's real state from the headset's videos: the recorder writes a file's `mdat` box only at its stop, so a file without one that was written in the last 20 s is being recorded.
- A start while such a file exists refuses with "The headset's own recorder records already": a recording of the headset's own menu runs. A start counts only once its file shows. A file of an earlier name that grew counts too.
- A quit of the app, SIGTERM and SIGINT among them (an update of the app sends SIGTERM), stops the headset's recorder and the sound at once. The parts stay. The next start of the app saves the recording.
- After a kill or a crash of the app, the next start finds the journal, stops the headset's recorder if it still records and saves the recording. Where the Mac's sound ended with the app, the headset's own sound fills in.
- A cable that comes out during a recording leaves the headset recording: "The cable is out: the headset records on. The Mac saves the recording when the cable is back." The app calls the cable out after three looks 2 s apart miss the headset, since a change of the headset's USB functions hides it for a second or two. When the cable is back, the app stops the recorder and saves what it recorded. A link that ends while a recording runs keeps the gem red, and a press on it says that the Mac saves the recording when the link is back.
- When the final file cannot be made, the headset's file itself becomes the final file, with the headset's own sound, and the headset keeps its copy.
- A saving that fails, as a pull that brings fewer bytes, keeps the parts and the headset's copy and tries again at the next look, up to three times.

Two other ways were tried first:
- Android's MediaProjection, which needs no setting: Horizon OS shows a picker of its own ("Entire view, including physical surroundings" or "Just this window") at each start. After Share, the capture got no frame in 3 s while cranvr's immersive app ran.
- The display's mirror (`screenrecord`, SurfaceControl): it gives both eyes at 92 fps in the display's own space, with the lens warp and the color fringes of the panels. No lens model is public. The warp moves with the IPD wheel.

**The sound.** The Mac's sound replaces the recorder's own track: it has no loss and nothing of the headset's own sounds. While the picture records, a process of the host's binary records the Mac's sound with a Core Audio tap (`cranvr-host --record-sound`, `host/src/recording_sound.rs`). The recorder's own track holds what the headset played: the same sound, some tens of milliseconds later. At the stop `cranvr-host --mux-recording` (`host/src/recording_mux.rs`) finds the lag between the two sounds (`host/src/recording_align.rs`) and puts the Mac's sound where the user heard it. A game is often silent for a minute or more, in its menus or while it loads, so the search takes its windows from the whole recording: up to six windows of 8 s, each where the headset's loudness changes most in its part. Each window searches the whole Mac sound, in steps of 10 ms and then to the millisecond, so a wrong time of the start costs nothing. The windows that agree within 40 ms give a line: the Mac sound's time at the picture's start and how fast the two clocks part. The final sound follows that line. Which sound the file takes:
- the Mac's sound where it matched the headset's track. Where it does not cover the picture, the headset's track fills in;
- the headset's own track when the Mac's sound was silent and the headset heard sound, and when both had sound that did not match: the headset's track is in time with the picture for sure;
- the Mac's sound at the times of the start when the headset heard nothing to match;
- the headset's track as it is when cranvr has no System Audio Recording permission. The setting "Microphone in recordings" on the Sound page (off by default) adds the headset's microphone: the recording reads the host's microphone ring as a game does, so the host asks the headset for its microphone while the recording runs.

**The file.** The final file is `~/Movies/cranvr/cranvr-YYYY-MM-DD-HHMMSS.mp4`, named after the start's local time. It holds the recorder's HEVC picture as it is, with no new encode. Its sound is AAC. The Mac app shows "Saved:" with the file's name and Show in Finder. The console in the headset says "Saved: NAME" for 8 s. The line beside the gem says it for 3 s. A recording stops by itself when the Mac's free disk falls under 5 GB. The app keeps a line for each start, each saved file with its numbers and each failure in `recordings.log` in the cranvr folder. The numbers hold the picture's size and rate, the sound's source and offset, the windows that matched and how well, the clocks' drift, the loudness of the Mac's sound and of the headset's track (RMS and peak in dB, and the seconds with sound), the microphone's loudness, what the tap did (its starts, its gaps) and whether the file passed its check. `CRANVR_RECORDINGS` names another folder, for a test.

**Measured** on a Quest 3 on a desk (2026-11-27), with the Amber theme and the user's stream settings (200 Mbps, no foveation):
- The four looks (`scripts/gem-looks.sh`, the mean color of the gem's lit pixels in a screen capture): idle violet (179, 150, 202), pointed at green (124, 192, 165), recording red (179, 64, 70) and not possible a dim violet (92, 70, 127). The dim gem has 49% of the idle gem's light. A press on it showed the reason beside it. Two captures of the recording gem 0.7 s apart had a light of 89 and 137: the pulse.
- The idle gem in a game (`scripts/ui3d-budget.sh` with `game:3d:holding` against `game:3d:holding:nogem`, 5 s each, twice): the compositor's time rose from 0.85 to 1.03 ms. The app's GPU time rose from 0.96 to 1.06 ms. The gem costs 0.29 ms in all. It adds two layers: its panel and the volumes' projection layer, whose images take 280 x 294 pixels of each eye's 1680 x 1760. With no left controller (`game:3d:none` against `game:3d:none:nogem`) both times stayed at 0.86 and 0.96 ms: a hidden gem costs nothing.
- A recording through the gem with a test app (`GAME=1 scripts/gem-check.sh`, the fake game at 120 fps, the virtual room): the stream held 120 fps on the Mac and on the headset while the headset's recorder ran. The compositor's time rose from 1.07 to 1.48 ms. The app's GPU time rose from 0.51 to 0.64 ms. The headset's stale frames rose from 0 to 5 in 6 s. Pose to display read 45 and 58 ms before the start and 43 and 44 ms while the recorder ran (one run). The Mac's state said "runs" 0.85 s after the press. The file was in its folder 2.1 s after the press that stopped a recording of 9 s.
- A recording through the gem with the installed app, in the virtual room, with a tone of 880 Hz from the Mac: the file held HEVC at 1920 x 1080 and 60 fps (544 frames in 9.1 s) and AAC at 48 kHz in stereo. The Mac's sound matched the headset's own track at 0.99. The tone peaked at -17 dB in the file. A frame 3 s in showed one view: its halves differed by 30 of 255 on average. 90% of that frame was not black.

- The Cyan theme's looks (`THEME=cyan scripts/gem-looks.sh`): idle blue (74, 179, 199), pointed at green (126, 191, 165), recording red (181, 67, 72) with a pulse of light 91 to 131, not possible a dim blue (26, 97, 127) at 53% of the idle gem's light.
- The recording's paths with a test app and Beat Saber's own song as the Mac's sound, through the headset (`scripts/recording-check.sh`, 2026-11-27): the hand menu's row started a recording that the Mac reported 1.7 s after the press, and its Stop saved the file 1.8 s after that press. A start while the headset's own recorder recorded refused at once. A free disk that read 4.5 GB stopped the recording within 2 s and saved it. A cable that read as pulled gave its note after three looks (7 s), a press gave it again, and the return of the cable saved the recording 2 s later. SIGTERM stopped the headset's recorder before the app ended, and the next start saved the recording 1 s after it began. After SIGKILL the next start found the headset's recorder still recording, stopped it and saved the recording.
- The Mac's sound matched the headset's track at 0.92, 0.96 and 0.99 in recordings of 5 to 13 s. The start's times alone were off by 33, 37 and 82 ms. A recording that the headset's own recorder had started 3 s before the Mac's sound took the headset's track for those 3 s.
- The headset's recorder's file holds 72 bytes for about 5 s after the start, then grows in pieces of about 2 MB. Until the stop its header holds only `ftyp` and `free` boxes; the stop writes the `mdat` header. The app reads that header to know the recorder's real state.

`scripts/gem-looks.sh` checks the gem's four looks on a Quest on a desk with a bare test host, with no recording. `scripts/recording-check.sh` checks the recording's paths on a Quest on a desk with a test app and real game sound: the hand menu's row, a start while the headset records, the microphone, the 5 GB stop, a cable that reads as pulled, SIGTERM and SIGKILL to the app. `scripts/gem-check.sh` makes a short recording through the gem with a test app and measures what the recording costs the stream. `scripts/recording-mux-check.sh` checks the final file with made-up parts on the Mac.
